Missoula Meshtastic Field Study

Phase 3 — October 9, 2026

Project: Missoula Meshtastic Field Study
Location: Missoula and Arlee, Montana
Radios: Ronin07 (fixed base), Shino07 (mobile)
Status: Preliminary field observations

Project Background

This report documents the third phase of field testing conducted October 7–9, 2026, using Heltec-based Meshtastic radios operating in the US 915 MHz band.

Testing evaluates practical communications coverage, mesh routing, RF propagation, antenna installation, and message acknowledgment behavior.

Phase 1 — October 7

The original baseline established a 15-location test route throughout Missoula.

Documented locations include:

* Location 2: 927 North Davis Street
* Location 3: Albertsons, North Reserve Street
* Additional locations throughout the Missoula area

The baseline recorded successful acknowledgments, failed retransmissions, mesh-node observations, and approximate geographic positions.

Phase 2 — October 8

The testing route was repeated with changes to radio configuration and antenna placement.

Two radios were used:

* Ronin07 — Fixed home station
* Shino07 — Mobile station

Observations included multi-hop mesh communication, node discovery, and message delivery behavior.

Phase 3 — October 9

The complete Ronin07 radio assembly was relocated above the roofline/chimney cap to improve RF exposure.

Shino07 remained the mobile test station.

The original route was repeated to evaluate changes in communications performance.

Additional measurements were gathered while traveling and during stationary testing outside the original baseline route.

Arlee, Montana observations:

* Both mobile and stationary operating conditions were examined.
* A stationary observation continued for over one hour.
* Screenshots recorded mesh activity and node information.
* Message visibility and actual destination acknowledgment require separate evaluation.

Power Interruption

A possible power interruption was identified during an earlier test sequence involving the original 15 locations.

That interruption may have affected continuity of communications and the completeness of the earlier results.

It should not be confused with later Arlee field observations.

Technical Findings

1. Mesh-node discovery does not necessarily establish message delivery.
2. Intermediate-node acknowledgment and destination acknowledgment are distinct events.
3. Antenna elevation and surrounding obstructions are significant variables.
4. Terrain and network participation may influence successful delivery.
5. Power continuity must be considered when evaluating failed or missing transmissions.

Preliminary Conclusions

Phase 3 establishes a new rooftop installation configuration and adds additional stationary and mobile observations to the earlier baseline.

A final location-by-location success comparison remains pending until message acknowledgment evidence, timestamps, and power-state observations are reconciled.

Future Work

* Compare October 7, 8, and 9 observations.
* Develop terrain-based RF coverage comparisons.
* Evaluate antenna placement and additional elevation.
* Investigate mesh routing and multi-hop behavior.
* Integrate Raspberry Pi monitoring and logging.
* Coordinate findings with the Open Device Bridge (ODB) project.

Project Relationships

The Meshtastic field study provides real-world operational evidence supporting the Open Device Bridge initiative.

ODB addresses hardware access, device configuration, flashing, recovery, and cross-platform compatibility challenges involving iPadOS, legacy Windows, and Raspberry Pi systems.
